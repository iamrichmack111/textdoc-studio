TITLE: Witchcraft, Folk Magic, and the Black American Experience

Across generations, Black communities in the United States have preserved a wide range of spiritual and folk traditions. Their histories are distinct, and terms such as Hoodoo, rootwork, Vodou, and modern witchcraft should not be treated as interchangeable.

A careful documentary begins with those distinctions. It asks where practices developed, how communities described them, and how outsiders represented them.

The story is also a story about survival, religion, family knowledge, migration, and cultural change. Historical claims should be tied to reliable sources rather than folklore presented as fact.
